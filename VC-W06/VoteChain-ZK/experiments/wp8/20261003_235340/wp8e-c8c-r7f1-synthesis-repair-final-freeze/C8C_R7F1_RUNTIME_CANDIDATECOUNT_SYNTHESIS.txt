GAS INTERPRETATION
------------------
K2 used 1,866,402 gas for every measured observation. K4, K8, and K16 each used 1,866,414 gas for every measured observation.

Therefore the full range across per-level mean gas values is 12 gas, and within-level gas sample SD is zero at every runtime level.

K2 calldata contained 10 zero bytes and 2,330 nonzero bytes; K4, K8, and K16 each contained 9 zero bytes and 2,331 nonzero bytes, while total calldata remained 2,340 bytes.

The only observed gas separation is therefore a 12-gas difference that coincides with the one-byte zero/nonzero calldata-composition difference. This benchmark does not establish that 12 gas as an isolated candidateCount opcode cost.

No additional transaction-gas separation is visible among K4, K8, and K16 despite their different runtime candidateCount values.

LOCAL LATENCY INTERPRETATION
----------------------------
Per-level mean local receipt latencies were: K2=865.021110 ms, K4=874.816695 ms, K8=864.572260 ms, and K16=863.063140 ms.

The range across these four means is 11.753555 ms. The means are neither monotonically increasing nor monotonically decreasing with runtime candidateCount.

Execution-position mean latencies ranged from 861.128355 ms to 873.424150 ms, a 12.295795 ms range. These position diagnostics are descriptive only.

The latency metric is local-process/RPC/instant-Anvil receipt latency. It includes local cast-process startup, RPC, instant mining, and receipt return. It excludes deployment, configure/activate, proof generation, calldata precomputation, and post-receipt reads.

PUBLICATION-SAFE CONCLUSION
---------------------------
In this controlled local fresh-chain benchmark, increasing runtime candidateCount from 2 to 16 did not produce a monotonic increase in accepted-vote gas or local receipt latency.

Gas was identical at K=4, K=8, and K=16 and was 12 gas lower at K=2; that K2 gas difference coincided with one additional zero calldata byte. Local mean latency varied non-monotonically within an 11.753555 ms range.

These measurements characterize transaction-level sensitivity under four frozen runtime election configurations. They do not isolate the cost of candidateCount itself because changing K also changes the indexed candidate-manifest hash, election-context hash, proof/public values, and calldata byte composition, while the active vote path continues to carry fixed-size 16-slot ciphertext arrays.

GUARDRAILS
----------
No inferential regression or causal candidateCount-opcode estimate was performed.

No outlier deletion, silent retry, replacement observation, pilot reuse, or warmup inclusion was used.

These local latency figures must not be compared directly with the published biometric/IoT end-to-end 10–15 second timing because the measurement scopes and environments differ.

The gas figures must not be presented as isolated ZKP overhead relative to the pre-ZKP system because the transaction protocol and contract logic differ.
